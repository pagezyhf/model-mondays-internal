# Now in Foundry: Tongyi-MAI Z-Image-Turbo, with FLUX.1-schnell and SDXL base 1.0

Open-source text-to-image generation has been moving in two directions at once: bigger transformer backbones for prompt fidelity, and aggressive distillation for sub-second inference on commodity hardware. This week's edition pairs a new arrival in Microsoft Foundry with two catalog mainstays to show where step-efficient image generation stands today—and how the open-source landscape has compounded into a usable, deployable stack.

This week's Model Mondays edition highlights three Hugging Face models in Microsoft Foundry: **Tongyi-MAI's Z-Image-Turbo**, a newly available 6B Diffusion Transformer (DiT) distilled to run in 8 steps with sub-second latency on a single GPU and native bilingual text rendering; **Black Forest Labs' FLUX.1-schnell**, a 12B rectified flow transformer distilled to 1–4 step inference and one of the most adopted open-weight image models since its 2024 release; and **Stability AI's stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0 (SDXL)**, the dual text encoder latent diffusion baseline that anchors a large ecosystem of LoRAs, ControlNets, and downstream fine-tunes.

## Models of the week

---

### [Tongyi-MAI: Z-Image-Turbo](https://ai.azure.com/catalog/models/tongyi-mai-z-image-turbo)

**Model Specs**
- Parameters / size: 6B (BF16)
- Resolution: Up to 1024×1024 native
- Primary task: Text-to-image generation (English and Chinese)

**Why it's interesting (Spotlight)**
- **Scalable Single-Stream Diffusion Transformer (S3-DiT) architecture**: Z-Image concatenates text tokens, visual semantic tokens, and image VAE tokens into a single unified input stream rather than running text and image through separate branches. This single-stream design improves parameter efficiency relative to dual-stream DiT architectures at the same capacity, which is part of why a 6B model competes with substantially larger systems on human preference evaluations. See the [Z-Image technical report](https://huggingface.co/papers/2511.22699) for details.
- **8-step inference at sub-second latency, fits in 16GB VRAM**: Z-Image-Turbo is distilled with Decoupled Distribution Matching Distillation (Decoupled-DMD) and further refined with DMDR, a method that fuses DMD with reinforcement learning during post-training. The result is a model that runs 8 Number-of-Function-Evaluations (NFE) per image with no Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)—which roughly halves the per-step compute compared to CFG-based inference—and reaches sub-second generation latency on enterprise GPUs while remaining within the memory budget of consumer-grade hardware. See the [Decoupled-DMD](https://huggingface.co/papers/2511.22677) and [DMDR](https://huggingface.co/papers/2511.13649) papers.
- **Native bilingual text rendering and strong instruction adherence**: Unlike most open-weight image models, which struggle with legible in-image text, Z-Image-Turbo renders complex English and Chinese text accurately—useful for posters, signage, packaging mockups, and marketing creative. On the Elo-based Human Preference Evaluation at the [Alibaba AI Arena](https://aiarena.alibaba-inc.com/corpora/arena/leaderboard?arenaType=T2I), Z-Image-Turbo achieves the top ranking among open-source models.

![Z-Image-Turbo Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/Tongyi-MAI/Z-Image-Turbo/resolve/main/assets/leaderboard.png)

**Try it**

Retail marketing scenario: You're building an in-house creative pipeline for a multinational consumer brand that ships campaigns simultaneously in English and Chinese markets. Deploy Z-Image-Turbo in Microsoft Foundry behind a managed endpoint and wire it into your campaign tooling. Given a product brief—seasonal theme, product SKU, primary headline copy in both languages—have the model generate a set of square and portrait creative variants suitable for social and storefront placements, with the bilingual headline rendered directly into the image. Use the model's 8-step inference to iterate through dozens of layout and palette variants per brief at interactive speed, then route the shortlist to your creative review queue.

### [Black Forest Labs: FLUX.1-schnell](https://ai.azure.com/catalog/models/black-forest-labs-flux-1-schnell)

**Model Specs**
- Parameters / size: 12B (rectified flow transformer)
- Resolution: Flexible up to 2 megapixels
- Primary task: Text-to-image generation

**Why it's interesting (Spotlight)**
- **Rectified flow transformer with adversarial distillation for 1–4 step inference**: FLUX.1-schnell is the distilled, Apache 2.0 sibling of the FLUX.1 family. It uses a rectified flow formulation (a diffusion variant that learns straight-line probability paths between noise and data, reducing the number of solver steps needed) and is further compressed with latent adversarial diffusion distillation. The model generates competitive-quality images in as few as 1 step and saturates around 4 steps, making it a strong fit for latency-sensitive workloads.
- **Permissive licensing for commercial use**: Released under Apache 2.0, FLUX.1-schnell can be used for personal, scientific, and commercial purposes without the additional license terms that govern the larger FLUX.1 [dev] and [pro] variants. This has driven broad adoption across product features that need an open, redistributable image backbone.
- **Strong prompt adherence at its parameter range**: At 12B parameters, FLUX.1-schnell sits between the SDXL family and frontier proprietary image models, and it remains a common reference point for evaluating open image generation prompt following—particularly for complex compositional prompts and longer captions—roughly two years after its initial release.

![FLUX.1-schnell sample grid](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell/resolve/main/schnell_grid.jpeg)

**Try it**

Ad creative iteration scenario: You're operating an ad performance team that runs dozens of paid social campaigns and needs continuous creative refresh to fight ad fatigue. Deploy FLUX.1-schnell in Microsoft Foundry and connect it to your creative ops system. For each campaign, take the current top-performing concept, generate 16 prompt variations that hold the subject and brand cues constant while varying lighting, background, and composition, and produce a 1-step preview render for each. Promote the strongest variants to a 4-step high-fidelity render and push them into your creative review tool with metadata linking each asset back to its source prompt for traceable A/B testing.

### [Stability AI: stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0](https://ai.azure.com/catalog/models/stabilityai-stable-diffusion-xl-base-1-0)

**Model Specs**
- Parameters / size: 2.6B UNet (≈3.5B total with text encoders)
- Resolution: 1024×1024 native
- Primary task: Text-to-image generation

**Why it's interesting (Spotlight)**
- **Dual text encoder design and an ensemble-of-experts pipeline**: SDXL uses two pretrained text encoders—OpenCLIP-ViT/G and CLIP-ViT/L—concatenated to capture both broad semantic alignment and finer-grained token-level cues. It can be run standalone or paired with the [SDXL refiner](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-refiner-1.0) in an ensemble-of-experts pipeline where the base model handles early denoising and the refiner specializes in the final steps. See the [SDXL report](https://huggingface.co/papers/2307.01952) for the original training and architecture details.
- **The most adopted open-weight image generation baseline**: With over 2 million downloads on the Hub, SDXL base 1.0 has the largest installed community of LoRAs, ControlNets, IP-Adapters, and domain fine-tunes of any open-weight image model. This ecosystem makes it the default starting point for teams that need to inject brand-specific styles, character identities, or controlled composition without training a model from scratch.
- **CreativeML Open RAIL++-M licensing for managed deployments**: SDXL is distributed under the CreativeML Open RAIL++-M license, which permits commercial use and downstream fine-tuning with documented use restrictions. Combined with its mature tooling support across diffusers, ONNX, and OpenVINO, SDXL remains a reliable baseline to deploy when production constraints favor a well-understood model over the latest architecture.

**Try it**

Ecommerce product imagery scenario: You're running a catalog operations team for an online retailer with thousands of SKUs and a steady inflow of new products that need on-brand imagery. Deploy SDXL base 1.0 in Microsoft Foundry with a brand-specific LoRA already fine-tuned on your existing studio shots. Given a structured product record—category, color, material, target environment—have the model generate a consistent set of lifestyle and white-background variants for each SKU, applying the brand LoRA at inference time. Pair the base model with the SDXL refiner endpoint for the final denoising steps on hero shots, and reserve standalone base inference for the high-volume long tail.
